# Theme Park Design Is Outdated — Outline

**Thesis:** Queues ration ride capacity with guests' time. Reservations can ration the same capacity with a schedule, which frees guests' hours. Parks already have the technology but use it mostly to charge for skipping lines. A park built around "how do we give every guest their best day" would spend less on queues and more on rides, rest, and cheap entertainment to fill the freed time.

**Core argument to repeat throughout:** A queue doesn't create capacity. It decides who rides by who will stand there longest. Swap it for a schedule and the same number of people ride without the waiting.

---

## 1. Hook: The line is the park's oldest attraction

- Open on the scene: hot pavement, switchbacks, a sign reading "60 min from this point." Queues have been part of the park day since Disneyland opened in 1955.
- Industry estimates put a typical guest at 2–3 hours of queuing per day ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/theme-park-interactive-installation-guide); a vendor figure, so use it with care).
- Guests rank long queues as the worst part of theme parks: 50% in the US, 70% in the UK ([accesso](https://accesso.com/learn/virtual-queuing-less-waiting-more-fun/)).
- Turn: we put up with it because it's always been this way. It doesn't need to be.

## 2. The technology already exists, and parks keep taking the free version away

This timeline is your strongest evidence. It shows that parks have built and run free reservation systems, then turned them into paid products.

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1999 | Disney's paper FastPass is **free** for all guests |
| 2013 | FastPass+: **free**, pre-booked through the app and MagicBands |
| 2017 | Disneyland swaps FastPass for paid **MaxPass** |
| 2017 | Volcano Bay opens with **no physical lines**. A TapuTapu wristband holds your place for every slide |
| 2021 | FastPass+ retired; paid **Genie+ / Lightning Lane** launch ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastpass)) |
| 2023 | Tokyo Disney brings back a **free** Priority Pass ([WDWNT](https://wdwnt.com/2026/05/tokyo-disney-resort-ending-free-priority-pass-introduced-for-40th-anniversary/)) |
| Oct 2025 | Volcano Bay **ends** TapuTapu virtual lines, with no reason given ([Inside Universal](https://www.insideuniversal.net/2025/04/universal-volcano-bay-to-discontinue-taputapu-and-virtual-lines-beginning-oct-1-2025/)) |
| Aug 2026 | Tokyo's free Priority Pass **ends**, leaving only paid Premier Access ([Disney Food Blog](https://www.disneyfoodblog.com/2026/07/31/well-another-disney-park-is-getting-rid-of-free-fastpasses/)) |

- **How much the paid version earns:** a leaked spreadsheet showed Genie+ brought in **$724M in pretax revenue at Walt Disney World alone** between Oct 2021 and Jun 2024 ([Fox 5 DC / WSJ](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/disney-leak-nullbulge-genie-plus-cruise-line)). Chapek said up to 50% of guests were buying it ([WDWMagic](https://wdwmagic.com/other/disney-genie/news/09feb2022-up-to-50-percent-of-walt-disney-world-park-guests-are-upgrading-to-disney-genie-according-to-bob-chapek.htm)).
- **Current prices:** Multi Pass runs about $25–42 per day. Premier Pass reaches **$449** per person at Magic Kingdom ([WDWMagic](https://www.wdwmagic.com/other/disney-genie/news/13mar2026-disney-world-lightning-lane-premier-pass-spring-break-2026-prices,-sellouts,-and-what-to-know-before-you-buy.htm)).
- The point: the reservation system works. Parks have chosen to sell it as a way out of a line they created.

## 3. The proposal: every ticket comes with reservations

Assumptions for the example: a $100 ticket, 30-minute standby waits, $20 per paid line-skip.

| | Today: standard | Today: premium | Proposed: standard | Proposed: premium |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spend | $100 | $100 + 5 × $20 = **$200** | $100 | $100 + 3 × $33 = **$199** |
| Rides | 8 | 11 (6 standby + 5 skip) | 8 | 11 |
| Time in line | **4 h** (8 × 30 min) | **3 h** (6 × 30 min) | **~0** | **~0** |

- Revenue stays about the same and every guest rides the same number of times. The change is that roughly 3–4 hours per guest stop going to standing in line.
- **The example is capacity-neutral.** Guests take the same number of rides as today, so the park needs no new ride capacity. Say this outright, because it's what stops the idea from sounding like a fantasy.
- How it works: you list the rides you want, a scheduler assigns return times, and it reschedules automatically when a ride breaks down or you're running late. Keep a small buffer queue (5–10 min) at each ride so it loads full.

## 4. Rebuttal: "Parks need lines so people will pay to skip them"

- That's a business model built on making guests' day worse. It only works as long as no competitor offers something better.
- **Free time turns into spending.** In an Omnico survey, 75% of US guests said they sometimes skip food and drink because of lines, and 65% skip merchandise ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/75-of-us-theme-park-visitors-wont-buy-food-and-beverage-if-there-are-queues-finds-omnico-664140953.html)). A guest standing in a queue can't buy anything.
- **The queue itself costs money to build.** Modern headliner queues are large, climate-controlled, and themed as carefully as the ride. Rise of the Resistance sits in a 165,000 sq ft building, and much of it is queue and pre-show ([MiceChat](https://www.micechat.com/244798-star-wars-rise-of-the-resistance-not-so-spoiler-y-walkthrough/)). Efteling's Danse Macabre queue winds through cloisters, a garden, a graveyard, and a forest before the show building ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danse_Macabre_(Efteling))).
- **Queues also need upkeep.** Disneyland's rebuilt Haunted Mansion queue was reported to have broken props soon after it opened ([Disney Dining](https://www.disneydining.com/new-haunted-mansion-queue-already-falling-apart-fans-are-furious-em1/)).
- Shrink the queue to a buffer and that land and money can go into more ride capacity, which is the one thing that actually reduces rationing.

## 5. Guests should be allowed to rest

- Reframed from the original "parks never let guests lie down." See the editor's notes.
- **Evidence:** about 8.5% of guests leave unhappy because of physical discomfort, and benches and shade are what they ask for most ([Katapult](https://www.katapult.co.uk/top-10-reasons-why-theme-park-guests-leave-unhappy)). Fans regularly ask for more shaded seating at Disney ([Inside the Magic](https://insidethemagic.net/2022/08/guests-want-benches-shade-disney-world-jc1/)), and some think the lack of seating is deliberate, to push guests into shops ([WDWInfo](https://www.wdwinfo.com/walt-disney-world/disney-debate-do-the-usa-disney-parks-have-enough-seating/)).
- **Argument:** a guest resting on a lawn costs the park almost nothing. A guest on a ride uses an expensive ride system. Rest is the cheapest way to fill time.
- **Precedents:** Tivoli Gardens and Efteling have parklike grounds. Cruise ships have thousands of loungers.

## 6. "Then guests will just be waiting for their reservations"

- No. Parks should fill the gaps with cheap, unlimited-capacity entertainment, themed to the same standard as the rest of the park.
- **Examples:** bowling, arcades, axe throwing, climbing structures and jungle gyms, walk-through experiences in the style of Meow Wolf, mini golf, splash pads, and lawns.
- **Psychology:** occupied time feels shorter than empty time, and waiting with a known end feels shorter than waiting with no end in sight (Maister, *The Psychology of Waiting Lines*, 1985, [davidmaister.com](https://davidmaister.com/articles/5/52/index.html)). A reservation provides both.
- **Combine the two:** put the play areas inside ride buildings, so your phone tells you when to walk from the play zone to the ride.

## 7. Cruise lines already work this way

- Royal Caribbean's app takes reservations for shows and activities. If reserved guests don't show, standby guests fill the seats ([Royal Caribbean FAQ](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/what-reservations-can-be-made-with-the-app)).
- The design pattern: a few capacity-limited headline experiences such as shows, slides, and the FlowRider, plus a much larger set of unlimited amenities like pools, loungers, trivia, bars, mini golf, and the arcade. Nobody stands in line for a pool.
- **Be honest about the limits:** Icon of the Seas still has waterslide lines of 45 minutes or more, plus crowding complaints ([Cruise Fever](https://cruisefever.net/is-icon-of-the-seas-too-crowded-passengers-fresh-off-the-ship-weigh-in/), [CruiseHive](https://www.cruisehive.com/royal-caribbean-guests-frustrated-with-water-slide-closures-on-icon-class-ships/205889)). The lesson to take from cruises is the ratio of headline experiences to unlimited amenities, not a claim that cruises have no waiting.

## 8. Close: design for the guest's day, not the queue's length

- Go back to the opening line. Picture a day that's all rides, play, food, and rest, with no time spent in line.
- Make the challenge to parks concrete: include reservations with every ticket, sell extra rides instead of line-skips, and build places to play and rest instead of bigger queues.

## Counterpoints to address in the article

1. **Headliner demand is larger than capacity, with or without a queue.** Seven Dwarfs Mine Train handles about 1,500 riders/hr, or roughly 21,000 a day ([Theme Park Tourist](https://www.themeparktourist.com/features/20170730/33009/which-magic-kingdom-rides-serve-most-customers)). Magic Kingdom averages about 48,800 guests a day ([Fox 13 / TEA](https://www.fox13news.com/news/magic-kingdom-park-most-visited-park-worldwide)), so at most about 43% of guests can ride it. Unplanned downtime of 10–17% lowers that further ([TouringPlans](https://touringplans.com/magic-kingdom/attractions/seven-dwarfs-mine-train/wait-times/date/2024-04-24)). **Rebuttal:** the queue doesn't solve this either; it just leaves 57% of guests disappointed after a long wait. A reservation system tells guests up front, can allocate headliners fairly (lottery, priority for multi-day guests), and the money saved on queues can fund more capacity.
2. **Free FastPass+ was unpopular too.** Guests disliked booking 60 days ahead, said it killed spontaneity, and disputed whether standby got longer ([AllEars reviews](https://allears.net/reviews/fastpass/page/11/), [Theme Park Insider](https://themeparkinsider.com/flume/201503/4453)). **Rebuttal:** design for same-day booking and automatic rescheduling, not a planning race months ahead.
3. **Volcano Bay tried a park with no lines and dropped it.** Virtual waits for Krakatau still reached hours ([TouringPlans](https://touringplans.com/blog/volcano-bay-lines-waves-waturi/)). Universal gave no reason, and coverage speculates about Express Pass sales ([Disney Food Blog](https://disneyfoodblog.com/2025/04/29/universals-volcano-bay-will-discontinue-virtual-queue-beginning-this-october)). **Rebuttal:** a virtual queue without enough else to do just moves the wait somewhere else, which is the point of Section 6. The change may also have been about revenue, which supports the thesis.
4. **Walkways and food outlets weren't built for everyone to be out of line at once.** Parks are currently designed on the assumption that a large share of guests are standing in queues. **Rebuttal:** that's exactly why they need more places to absorb people: play areas, lawns, and bigger seating zones.
5. **Themed queues are part of the experience.** Pre-shows tell the ride's story. **Rebuttal:** keep the pre-shows and build them sized as short story rooms, not as long holding pens.
6. **Indoor arcades have failed before.** DisneyQuest closed in Chicago in 2001 and Orlando in 2017 after attendance fell ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DisneyQuest)). **Rebuttal:** DisneyQuest was a separate ticketed venue whose technology was never updated. Play inside the park, filling gaps between reservations, is a different use case.
7. **Paid line-skipping makes a lot of money.** Disney credited Genie+ for higher per-guest spending ([WDWNT](https://wdwnt.com/2022/02/average-per-ticket-spending-increases-at-disney-parks-due-partially-to-genie-and-lightning-lane)). **Rebuttal:** the Section 3 example keeps that revenue by selling extra rides instead of line-skips.

## Editor's notes (pushback on the brief)

- **"Most people know what rides they want and don't care about order."** I found nothing to support this, and the FastPass+ backlash suggests guests dislike rigid schedules. Recommend rewording to "guests care about *which* rides, not *when*, as long as the system adapts on the day."
- **"It should not be difficult to schedule everyone to do everything."** The capacity numbers above show this isn't true for headliners. Make the stronger claim instead: queues already fail to fit everyone in, and reservations fail more honestly and without the wait.
- **"Parks never allow guests to lie down."** I found no written rule against it. Disney's posted rules cover running and clothing, not lying down ([Disneyland rules](https://disneyland.disney.go.com/park-rules/)). The claim you can support is that parks provide too little rest and shade. I've reframed Section 5 on that basis, and it could be merged into Section 6.
- **"Queues are built as large as the ride and themed to the same standard."** This is plausible, but I found no public cost breakdown. Either soften it ("often a large share of the building") or find an Imagineer interview or a TEA source.
- **The cruise comparison** needs the multi-day caveat: on a 7-day sailing, demand for each attraction is spread over a week. Keep the comparison but frame it as a design ratio, not "no lines."
- **Vendor statistics** (spending lifts of 18–25%, 84% preferring virtual queues) are unsourced marketing. I've left them out; use the Omnico survey instead.
