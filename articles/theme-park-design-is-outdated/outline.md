# Theme Park Design Is Outdated — Outline

**Thesis:** Queues ration ride capacity with guests' time. Reservations can ration the same capacity with a schedule, which frees guests' hours. Parks already have the technology but use it mostly to charge for skipping lines. A park built around "how do we give every guest their best day" would spend less on queues and more on rides, rest, and cheap entertainment to fill the freed time.

**Core argument to repeat throughout:** A queue doesn't create capacity. It decides who rides by who will stand there longest. Swap it for a schedule and the same number of people ride without the waiting.

**Running metric: dollars per enjoyment.** This is construction and operating cost divided by the enjoyment it produces (guest-hours × how much guests like those hours). Use it to compare everything a park builds:

| What the park builds | Cost | Enjoyment | Dollars per enjoyment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ride | Very high | Very high | Good |
| Themed queue | High (often a big share of the building) | Negative when long, wasted when short | **Terrible** |
| Lawn or shaded seating | Very low | Moderate | **Excellent** |
| Arcade, play area, walk-through | Low to moderate | High, unlimited capacity | **Excellent** |

## 1. Hook: The line is the park's oldest attraction

- Open on a Disneyland scene: a hot afternoon, switchbacks, a sign reading "75 min from this point." Queues have been part of the park day since Disneyland opened in 1955.
- **How much time it takes:** Rise of the Resistance at Disneyland averages about 61 minutes standby and peaks around 91 ([Disneyblog / Queue-Times](https://disneyblog.com/destinations/disneyland-resort/disneyland-park/star-wars-galaxys-edge/star-wars-rise-of-the-resistance-disneyland-park/)). Park-wide averages run about 25–30 minutes, but that includes walk-on rides ([ThemeParkIQ](https://www.themeparkiq.com/disneyland)). A guest who rides 8 popular rides at about 45 minutes each spends **about 6 hours in line**, which is half of a 12-hour day.
- Guests rank long queues as the worst part of theme parks: 50% in the US, 70% in the UK ([accesso](https://accesso.com/learn/virtual-queuing-less-waiting-more-fun/)).
- Turn: we put up with it because it's always been this way. It doesn't need to be.

## 2. The technology already exists, and parks keep taking the free version away

This timeline is your strongest evidence. It shows that parks have built and run free reservation systems, then turned them into paid products.

| Year | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1999 | Disney's paper FastPass is **free** for all guests |
| 2013 | FastPass+: **free**, pre-booked through the app and MagicBands |
| 2017 | Disneyland swaps FastPass for paid **MaxPass** |
| 2017 | Volcano Bay opens with **no physical lines**. A TapuTapu wristband holds your place for every slide |
| 2021 | FastPass+ retired; paid **Genie+ / Lightning Lane** launch ([Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fastpass)) |
| 2023 | Tokyo Disney brings back a **free** Priority Pass ([WDWNT](https://wdwnt.com/2026/05/tokyo-disney-resort-ending-free-priority-pass-introduced-for-40th-anniversary/)) |
| Oct 2025 | Volcano Bay **ends** TapuTapu virtual lines, with no reason given ([Inside Universal](https://www.insideuniversal.net/2025/04/universal-volcano-bay-to-discontinue-taputapu-and-virtual-lines-beginning-oct-1-2025/)) |
| Aug 2026 | Tokyo's free Priority Pass **ends**, leaving only paid Premier Access ([Disney Food Blog](https://www.disneyfoodblog.com/2026/07/31/well-another-disney-park-is-getting-rid-of-free-fastpasses/)) |

- **How much the paid version earns:** a leaked spreadsheet showed Genie+ brought in **$724M in pretax revenue at Walt Disney World alone** between Oct 2021 and Jun 2024 ([Fox 5 DC / WSJ](https://www.fox5dc.com/news/disney-leak-nullbulge-genie-plus-cruise-line)). Chapek said up to 50% of guests were buying it ([WDWMagic](https://wdwmagic.com/other/disney-genie/news/09feb2022-up-to-50-percent-of-walt-disney-world-park-guests-are-upgrading-to-disney-genie-according-to-bob-chapek.htm)).
- **Current prices:** Multi Pass runs about $25–42 per day. Premier Pass reaches **$449** per person at Magic Kingdom ([WDWMagic](https://www.wdwmagic.com/other/disney-genie/news/13mar2026-disney-world-lightning-lane-premier-pass-spring-break-2026-prices,-sellouts,-and-what-to-know-before-you-buy.htm)).
- The point: the reservation system works. Parks have chosen to sell it as a way out of a line they created.

## 3. The proposal: every ticket comes with reservations

The example uses round numbers for illustration.

| | Spend | Rides | Time in line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Today:** $100 ticket | $100 | 8 | ~6 h |
| **Today:** $100 ticket + 4 × $20 fast passes | $180 | 12 (8 standby + 4 skips) | ~6 h |
| **Proposed:** $100 ticket with 8 reservations included | $100 | 8 | **0** |
| **Proposed:** $100 ticket + 4 × $20 extra reservations | $180 | 12 | **0** |

- **The business model doesn't change.** It's the same ticket price, the same $20 add-on, and the same impulse purchase during the day, now sold as an extra ride instead of a line-skip. Revenue and ride counts stay the same. The only thing that disappears is the line, for everyone, including guests who buy nothing extra.
- **The example is capacity-neutral.** Guests take the same number of rides as today, so the park needs no new ride capacity. Say this outright, because it's what stops the idea from sounding like a fantasy.
- How it works: you list the rides you want, a scheduler assigns return times, and it reschedules automatically when a ride breaks down or you're running late. Keep a short buffer queue at each ride so it loads full. Keep pre-shows too, wherever they make the ride better.

## 4. Rebuttal: "Parks need lines so people will pay to skip them"

- That's a business model built on making guests' day worse.
- **Free time turns into spending.** In an Omnico survey, 75% of US guests said they sometimes skip food and drink because of lines, and 65% skip merchandise ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/75-of-us-theme-park-visitors-wont-buy-food-and-beverage-if-there-are-queues-finds-omnico-664140953.html)). A guest standing in a queue can't buy anything.
- **Queues have the worst dollars per enjoyment of anything in the park.** Modern headliner queues are large, climate-controlled, and themed as carefully as the ride. Rise of the Resistance sits in a 165,000 sq ft building, and much of it is queue and pre-show ([MiceChat](https://www.micechat.com/244798-star-wars-rise-of-the-resistance-not-so-spoiler-y-walkthrough/)).
- **Example: Harry Potter and the Forbidden Journey.** The queue is a walk through Hogwarts castle: the greenhouses, moving portraits, Dumbledore's office, the Defense Against the Dark Arts classroom, the Gryffindor common room, and the Sorting Hat ([Inside the Magic](https://insidethemagic.net/2010/05/tour-the-harry-potter-and-the-forbidden-journey-queue-in-the-wizarding-world/)). It's some of the most expensive theming in the park, and guests only ever see it in one of two bad ways:
  - **When the line is long,** the castle becomes a crowded waiting room you're stuck in.
  - **When the line is short,** you rush past everything. Team members hurry guests along ([TouringPlans](https://touringplans.com/blog/a-virtual-guided-walk-through-of-hogwarts/)), and guests "blew through" without studying the details ([MousePlanet](https://mouseplanet.com/muggle-borns-guide-to-harry-potter-and-the-forbidden-journey/3172)).
  - The same castle as a walk-through guests *choose* to do, at their own pace, is a strong attraction by itself.
- Shrink the queue to a short buffer and that land and money can go into more ride capacity. More capacity is the only thing that actually reduces rationing.

## 5. Guests should be allowed to rest

- **The design choice:** queues are standing-only rails, and parks make sure there's nowhere comfortable to lie down. Seating outside queues is scarce too.
- **Evidence:** about 8.5% of guests leave unhappy because of physical discomfort, and benches and shade are what they ask for most ([Katapult](https://www.katapult.co.uk/top-10-reasons-why-theme-park-guests-leave-unhappy)). Fans regularly ask for more shaded seating at Disney ([Inside the Magic](https://insidethemagic.net/2022/08/guests-want-benches-shade-disney-world-jc1/)), and some think the lack of seating is deliberate, to push guests into shops ([WDWInfo](https://www.wdwinfo.com/walt-disney-world/disney-debate-do-the-usa-disney-parks-have-enough-seating/)).
- **Dollars per enjoyment:** a guest resting on a lawn costs the park almost nothing. A guest on a ride uses an expensive ride system. Rest has the best ratio of anything the park can offer.
- **Precedents:** Tivoli Gardens and Efteling have parklike grounds. Cruise ships have thousands of loungers.

## 6. What will guests do instead of standing in line?

Frame this section as an opportunity, not a defense. Once guests aren't stuck in queues, they have hours free, and the park can fill them with experiences that cost far less than rides and have no capacity limit.

- **Where this already works:**
  - **Universal's interactive wands.** Guests walk the Wizarding World hunting for spell-casting spots. It's a paid add-on that turns a stroll into a game, and Universal keeps expanding it. Second-generation wands launched in 2025, and Epic Universe added 14 more casting spots ([Blooloop](https://blooloop.com/theme-park/news/universal-orlando-wizarding-world-harry-potter-new-interactive-wands/)).
  - **Sorcerers of the Magic Kingdom (2012–2021).** This was a free card game included with admission. Guests could play in 15–20 minute bursts between rides, or for 3–4 hours straight ([AllEars](https://allears.net/tp/mk/sorcerers-of-the-magic-kingdom.htm)).
  - **Meow Wolf.** A walk-through with no ride system: guests spend a median of about 2 hours inside ([Placer.ai](https://www.placer.ai/anchor/articles/cats-out-of-the-bag-unlocking-meow-wolfs-secrets)), and the Las Vegas location drew 1 million visitors in its first year ([Yahoo](https://news.yahoo.com/meow-wolf-las-vegas-nev-003500743.html)).
  - **Disneyland's own play spaces:** Pirate's Lair on Tom Sawyer Island and the Redwood Creek Challenge Trail at California Adventure, where kids burn hours on rope bridges and slides.
- **What to add:** bowling, arcades, axe throwing, climbing structures and jungle gyms, walk-throughs (like the Hogwarts castle in Section 4), mini golf, splash pads, and lawns. Theme them to the same standard as the rest of the park.
- **Psychology:** occupied time feels shorter than empty time, and waiting with a known end feels shorter than waiting with no end in sight (Maister, *The Psychology of Waiting Lines*, 1985, [davidmaister.com](https://davidmaister.com/articles/5/52/index.html)). A reservation provides both.
- **Combine the two:** put the play areas inside ride buildings, so your phone tells you when to walk from the play zone to the ride.

## 7. Cruise lines already work this way

- Royal Caribbean's app takes reservations for shows and activities. If reserved guests don't show, standby guests fill the seats ([Royal Caribbean FAQ](https://www.royalcaribbean.com/faq/questions/what-reservations-can-be-made-with-the-app)).
- The design pattern: a few capacity-limited headline experiences such as shows, slides, and the FlowRider, plus a much larger set of unlimited amenities like pools, loungers, trivia, bars, mini golf, and the arcade. Nobody stands in line for a pool.
- **Be honest about the limits:** Icon of the Seas still has waterslide lines of 45 minutes or more, plus crowding complaints ([Cruise Fever](https://cruisefever.net/is-icon-of-the-seas-too-crowded-passengers-fresh-off-the-ship-weigh-in/), [CruiseHive](https://www.cruisehive.com/royal-caribbean-guests-frustrated-with-water-slide-closures-on-icon-class-ships/205889)). On a 7-day sailing, demand for each attraction is also spread over a week. The lesson to take from cruises is the ratio of headline experiences to unlimited amenities, not a claim that cruises have no waiting.

## 8. Close: design for the guest's day, not the queue's length

- Go back to the opening line. Picture a day that's all rides, play, food, and rest, with no time spent in line.
- Make the challenge to parks concrete:
  - Include reservations with every ticket.
  - Sell extra rides instead of line-skips.
  - Spend the queue budget on places to play and rest.

## Counterpoints to address in the article

1. **Headliner demand is larger than capacity, with or without a queue.** Seven Dwarfs Mine Train handles about 21,000 riders a day ([Theme Park Tourist](https://www.themeparktourist.com/features/20170730/33009/which-magic-kingdom-rides-serve-most-customers)), while Magic Kingdom averages about 48,800 guests ([Fox 13 / TEA](https://www.fox13news.com/news/magic-kingdom-park-most-visited-park-worldwide)). At most about 43% of guests can ride it on a given day. Disneyland, at about 17.3M visitors a year or roughly 47,000 a day ([Fox LA / TEA](https://www.foxla.com/news/magic-kingdom-park-most-visited-park-worldwide)), has the same problem.
   - **Rebuttal:** a queue already makes this cut. It does it by who's willing to wait: some guests wait an hour, and others see the sign and give up. A reservation system makes the same cut without anyone losing an hour. It can also allocate fairly (a lottery, or priority for guests who haven't ridden yet), and the money saved on queues can fund more capacity.
2. **Free FastPass+ was unpopular too.** Guests disliked booking 60 days ahead and said it killed spontaneity ([AllEars reviews](https://allears.net/reviews/fastpass/page/11/), [Theme Park Insider](https://themeparkinsider.com/flume/201503/4453)).
   - **Rebuttal:** design for same-day booking and automatic rescheduling, not a planning race months ahead.
3. **Volcano Bay tried a park with no lines and dropped it.** Virtual waits for Krakatau still reached hours ([TouringPlans](https://touringplans.com/blog/volcano-bay-lines-waves-waturi/)). Universal gave no reason, and coverage speculates about Express Pass sales ([Disney Food Blog](https://disneyfoodblog.com/2025/04/29/universals-volcano-bay-will-discontinue-virtual-queue-beginning-this-october)).
   - **Rebuttal:** a virtual queue without enough else to do just moves the wait somewhere else, which is the point of Section 6. The change may also have been about revenue, which supports the thesis.
4. **Indoor arcades have failed before.** DisneyQuest closed in Chicago in 2001 and Orlando in 2017 after attendance fell ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DisneyQuest)).
   - **Rebuttal:** DisneyQuest was a separate ticketed venue whose technology was never updated. Play inside the park, filling gaps between reservations, is a different use case.
5. **Paid line-skipping makes a lot of money.** Disney credited Genie+ for higher per-guest spending ([WDWNT](https://wdwnt.com/2022/02/average-per-ticket-spending-increases-at-disney-parks-due-partially-to-genie-and-lightning-lane)).
   - **Rebuttal:** keep that revenue. The Section 3 example sells the same $20 add-on as an extra ride instead of a line-skip.

## Editor's notes

- **The 6-hour figure is our own estimate:** 8 rides at about 45 minutes. I found no published total for daily time in line at Disneyland. Present it as an illustration, or measure it on a real visit.
- **The claim that parks deliberately avoid comfortable places to lie down** is an observation, and I found no posted rule to cite. Photos from the park (standing-only rails, lean bars, no lawns) would back it up better than a citation. Note the exception: Rise of the Resistance has some seating in its queue for breakdowns ([MiceChat](https://www.micechat.com/244798-star-wars-rise-of-the-resistance-not-so-spoiler-y-walkthrough/)).
- **I found no public cost breakdown for queue construction.** The dollars-per-enjoyment table uses relative ratings, not real figures, so it doesn't need one.
- **"Guests know which rides they want, not when."** This holds only if the scheduler adapts on the day. The FastPass+ backlash shows guests dislike rigid schedules made far in advance.
- **Vendor statistics** (spending lifts of 18–25%, 84% preferring virtual queues) are unsourced marketing. I've left them out; use the Omnico survey instead.
